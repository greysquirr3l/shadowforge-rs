Extraction reverses the [embed pipeline](./embed-pipeline/), but it is not a mirror image. Covers
may be missing, corrupted, or processed by a platform in between, so the extraction side has to
tolerate partial failure rather than assume a clean round trip.

Partial failure is expected: covers may be missing, and individual shares may fail their integrity check.

## The stages

| Stage | What happens | Where |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| **gather** | Collect whichever covers are available | `application/services` |
| **decapsulate** | Recover the shared secret from the recipient's private key | `domain/crypto` |
| **decode** | Read the embedded share out of each cover | `domain/stego` |
| **reconstruct** | Discard shares that fail their HMAC tag, then rebuild from the remaining ones | `domain/reconstruction` |
| **decrypt** | Open the reconstructed ciphertext | `domain/crypto` |
| **verify** | Check the ML-DSA-87 signature | `domain/crypto` |

## Reconstruction tolerates missing covers

Reconstruction takes the shares it has and discards any whose HMAC-SHA-256 tag does not verify.
A share that was corrupted in transit — by a platform recompressing the carrier, for example —
fails the tag and is excluded. As long as at least K valid shares remain, the payload is
recovered exactly.

This is the mechanism that makes partial loss survivable rather than fatal. It is also why a
corrupted share can never silently poison the output: it is rejected before it reaches the
decoder.

## Failure is reported, not guessed

If fewer than K shares verify, or the signature does not check out, the extraction fails with an
error rather than returning a partial payload. A tool whose output might be silently truncated
is worse than useless for a source who has already decided what to publish.

## Cover techniques are pluggable

The `decode` stage is the only technique-specific part of the pipeline. Which technique was used
is chosen by the caller, not inferred from the cover — the same discipline the embed side
follows.
